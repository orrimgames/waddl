Concept v0.5Duckling companion

Meet Pip.

Pip is a palm-sized duckling robot. It waddles on two webbed feet, flaps tiny wings when it's happy, and looks at you through two glowing eyes on a dark glass face. One part pet, one part desk buddy, zero parts intimidating.

The idea

Every design decision is a fight between cute and cost. Cute wins ties.

The face is the product

A dark glass panel with two glowing eyes, driven by a cheap 0.96" OLED. Blinking, looking around, going wide when you pick Pip up. Eilik proved people bond with the face; everything else is supporting cast.

Waddle, don't walk

Walking robots need lots of servos. Waddling needs two: rock side to side, lift a foot, fall forward adorably. Duck anatomy is a cost-saving feature.

Wings for emotion

Two more servos in the wings. A happy flap, a shy droop, an angry flutter. Four servos total, and Pip can act.

No cloud, ever

Pip listens, thinks, and speaks entirely on-device. No account, no subscription, no server to shut down. When the company's gone, Pip still works. That's not a limitation - that's the brand.

Egg-simple shell

The body is basically an egg with feet. One main print, small add-ons, no exotic geometry. Any entry-level 3D printer can make a Pip.

The face, live

This is the eye logic from firmware/pip_main_v01.ino, running on this page. Pip idles, looks around, and blinks on its own - or pick a mood.

Specs, concept stage

ItemPip v0.5
Height~14 cm at print scale (cad/pip_v05.scad)
BrainESP32 dev board. Tier B adds a Pi Zero 2 W for real on-device conversation.
Muscles4x SG90 9g micro servos: 2 feet, 2 wings
Face0.96" OLED, 128x64, two animated eyes
VoicePiezo buzzer at Tier 0/A; real local speech (Piper TTS) at Tier B
Ears (Tier A)INMP441 mic + on-device keyword spotting (microWakeWord). "Hey Pip" plus commands like dance, spin, sleep - nothing leaves the robot. design
Power3.7V LiPo, USB-C charging module
ShellPLA, ~150g of filament, cream + orange
Build cost~$45 Tier 0/A, ~$82 conversational Tier B (full breakdown)
