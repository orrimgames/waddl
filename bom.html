Build steps

From box of parts to first waddle. Estimated a weekend.

  1. Print the shell. pip_body_shell.stl plus feet, wings, beak, tail as separate color parts. 0.2mm layers, no supports for the egg body. About 10-12 hours of print time.
  2. Seat the servos. Feet servos go in the lower body, horns down into each foot. Wing servos in the mid-body, horns out the sides. SG90s come with screws in the bag.
  3. Wire the brain. Servo signal wires to ESP32 GPIO (18/19 feet, 21/22 wings), OLED to I2C (SDA 23, SCL 25), buzzer to GPIO 26, LiPo through the TP4056 and switch to the 5V rail.
  4. Flash the firmware. Arduino IDE, ESP32Servo library. First sketch: center all servos, then the waddle loop - rock, lift left foot, rock, lift right foot.
  5. Wake the face. OLED eye animation loop: idle look-around, blink every few seconds, wide eyes on pickup (v0.2 adds a touch sensor).
  6. First waddle. Feet on desk, power on, film everything.

Known risks

Balance

Two-foot waddlers live or die by center of mass. The sim rocks Pip 8 degrees; the real gait gets tuned on the desk. Worst case: wider feet, lower battery.

Servo jitter on battery

Four SG90s pulling at once can brown out a weak rail. Fix is a capacitor on the 5V line and staggering servo moves in firmware.

Shell fit

Concept model has no mounts yet. v0.2 CAD adds servo pockets, a board tray, and screw bosses before the first print.

Scope

Camera, mic, app, AI voice - all deliberately NOT in v0. Pip earns features one prototype at a time.
