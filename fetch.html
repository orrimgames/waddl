How it works

Cheap eyes, dumb chassis. The expensive robots map the whole court with AI. Fetch just needs to see yellow.

It drives to balls

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W and one camera spot optic-yellow felt against the court - plain color detection, no cloud, no mapping rig. Works on hard courts and clay.

A hole in the front eats them

A motorized silicone roller pulls each ball in through the front intake and up a short chute. No arms, no vacuums, nothing to snap.

The top stores 80

A 20-liter top hopper holds a full teaching basket. The 12 Ah battery sits low to keep the loaded bot planted, with gyro-limited turns.

Why so cheap?

The $2,000 robots are priced by their sensors. A tennis court is a fenced rectangle with bright yellow balls on it - the last problem in robotics that actually needs a $1,000 vision stack.

  1. No mapping. Fetch sweeps rows and steers to what the camera sees. Bump switches handle the fence.
  2. No arm, no vacuum. One roller and gravity move the ball. Fewer parts, fewer failures.
  3. Two small boards. An ESP32 drives the motors, a Pi Zero 2 W sees. Together they cost less than the incumbents' shipping.
